The Golden Triangle

An interactive simulation of a Sime Darby township with three parts working together: Linear Green Spines, Dynamic IoT Road Networks and solar-powered e-bike stations.

Pillar 1: Linear Green Spines

Permeable pavers and bioswales absorb floodwater instead of relying only on drains.

Conventional road

Golden Triangle road

Red dashed line: water reaches the road and homes. Gold dashed line: sensor threshold where the automated gates close. The Golden Triangle road adds absorption through pavers and bioswales, plus gate control. The trees also shade the walkway, which makes short walks more comfortable.

Solar power

Solar panels power the water-level sensors and the e-bike charging stations.

Water-level sensors use very little power (about 0.5 W), so a small panel and a small battery can keep them running through storms and at night. Illustrative assumptions: a 5 W panel and a 5 Wh battery.

Pillar 2: Dynamic IoT Road Networks

Water-level sensors watch the bioswales and warn residents early. Optional DAS fibre-optic cables under the road listen for vibration changes that signal potholes.

Distributed Acoustic Sensing (DAS) cables are optional but efficient. They detect vibration changes under the road. Combined with bioswale soil moisture and pressure data, they predict potholes so crews can repair them off-peak.

How the sensors stay cool

Split-brain design. The sensing probe stays in the open and sends its readings down a cable to the microprocessor, which is buried underground where it stays cooler.

Venturi chimney effect. A hollow mini chimney with angled slats surrounds the sensor. When a breeze passes, the pressure drops inside and pulls hot air out. It only works while there is some wind.

Pillar 3: E-bike charging stations

Short trips by e-bike instead of by car. Residents unlock a bike free with their personal QR code in the app. Visitors pay RM 3 per 30 minutes first. Try taking and returning bikes.

Each station runs on its own solar panels and battery, so charging slows in a storm and the station runs on its battery at night. Open app.html to see a resident's QR code and ebike-rental.html for the visitor payment page. Station charge values are illustrative.
